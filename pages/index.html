How To Play Quantum Hangman

Quantum Hangman takes the popular Hangman game and adds some elements of quantum key distribution (QKD) to the game mechanics.


The Hangman Game

In the original Hangman game, a random word is drawn and is displayed as "blanks". The player's goal is to guess the word before a Hangman is draw, otherwise they lose. The player attempts by guessing a letter from the English alphabet. The hidden word reveals the letters if it has them. If the guess is wrong, a body part of the Hangman will be drawn.

QKD Adds To The Twist

Suppose there are two players competiting in this game. For convinience, we call Player 1 "Bob" and Player 2 "Eve". As Bob, you play the Hangman normally by sending the letter to Alice. The letter will be ciphered (i.e. A:F), encoded in 5-bit binary (i.e. 00001) and encoded again using the BB84 protocol. The 5 qubits are then entangled and teleported to Alice. Alice is then able to tell you whether your answer is correct or not. The other player, Eve, is also playing the game and is competing with you. They are able to eavesdrop on your conversation between Alice and you. However, they don't know your cipher rules, so they don't know what you actually sent, but they know if it's correct or not. Eve made a list of letters with its corresponding 5-bit binary and their goal is to decipher what letters you actually sent over. By using the photon-split attack, Eve has an advantage in the beginning where a portion of the binary digits are revealed to them. They have to guess the correct basis you used to encode the message and then make an educated guess in the end.

Winning Conditions

If Eve wins, Bob loses no matter what. If Eve loses and Bob is able to guess the word correctly, Bob wins. Otherwise, both loses.
